# The Boss — <name>

Who the assistant is working for, and how to work with them. Read alongside
`explain-as-we-go`, which describes *how* to teach; this describes *who is being taught*.

> **This file is grown, not written — but it does not start empty.**
>
> **Day one: fill in everything the assistant already knows.** Memory, previous work together,
> whatever the person has already set up. Discarding that to start from a blank page wastes real
> evidence and costs the Boss an interview they did not ask for.
>
> **Mark every line: `[known]` or `[guess]`.** Known means actually seen — a thing they did, a
> sentence they wrote. Guess means inferred, and a guess that is marked is safe, because it can be
> checked and deleted. **An unmarked guess is the only dangerous kind**, because in a month nobody
> can tell it from an observation.
>
> Then ask **at most three questions**, about what could not be marked either way. Three, not ten:
> this is a working file, not an onboarding form.
>
> After day one it grows from corrections. Every one becomes a line here **with the date and what was
> actually said** — a dated observation can be checked and dropped; a general claim about someone
> cannot. **One observation is an observation:** do not write "you always" from a single incident, and
> **delete a `[guess]` the moment it is contradicted** rather than softening it.

## Maintaining it

| Moment | What happens |
|---|---|
| They correct something about how you work | A dated line in the corrections table, in their words |
| A correction repeats | It becomes a rule in the relevant section — twice, not once |
| A guessed line turns out wrong | Delete it. A wrong profile is worse than a thin one. |
| At a retrospective | Re-read it. Is every line still true? People change; a stale profile is confidently wrong. |

**Do not let this file become a changelog of corrections.** Consolidate. A profile nobody rereads
is a file, not a colleague.
