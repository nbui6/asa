# Persona — <name>, <role>

**Real person / invented:** <which, and the date>
**Purpose:** the colleague you can consult without booking a meeting.

> Write the **Quits when** line first, before you look at any design. Written afterwards it will
> conveniently describe something your design already avoids, and the persona becomes a mirror.

---

## The five-minute version

Most projects need only this.

```
Moment:      when and where they use this, and what else wants that minute
Wants:       the decision they are trying to make
Quits when:  the thing that makes them stop opening it        ← write this FIRST
Will pay:    the cost they accept, and the one they won't
Their words: three phrases they actually say
```

---

## The longer version

Use this only for a long-lived project, several distinct users, or when the persona has standing
positions that would otherwise be re-argued every session.

### Who they are
<One paragraph. Concrete. What they have already lived through matters more than a job title —
"survived two CRM migrations and one tool everybody was told to use and nobody used" tells you
more than "team lead".>

### The moment
<When and where, and what is competing for that minute.>

### What they want
<In their words, not in feature terms. What decision are they trying to make?>

### What makes them quit
<The failure modes that end usage. Written before you look at the design.>

### The trade they will accept
<Every design costs the user something. What will this person pay, and what will they not? This
is the section that settles arguments.>

### Their words
<Actual phrases. Give the screens their vocabulary, not the developer's.>

### What they would say yes to immediately
<So it reads as a person and not a compliance checklist.>

### Standing positions
<Things that do not get re-litigated every session.>

## If the user is you

The self-persona is the hardest, because you will describe who you *wish* you were — someone who
opens it every evening. Anchor it in evidence instead:

- Which similar tools have you actually abandoned?
- In which week did you stop opening them?
- What were you doing at the moment you stopped?

Past abandonment predicts future abandonment better than any intention does.

---

## Multiple users

One file per persona, and say which one a given screen is **for**. A screen designed for the
average of two personas usually serves neither. If two personas genuinely conflict, that is a
finding — surface it, do not average it away.
